# Selling Digital Templates (Notion, Figma, Checklists/Worksheets, Etsy Printables, etc.), 2023-2026

## What documented, named, numbers-backed cases exist of real income from selling digital templates?

### Takeaway
There is a real, documented tier of named individuals earning meaningful-to-large money from templates (Notion in particular, also Etsy spreadsheets/printables), but nearly all of the well-verified, large cases involve a creator who first built a sizable audience (YouTube, Twitter/X, newsletter, Pinterest SEO) before or alongside selling — the product itself is rarely the growth engine. Below-the-headline, informal evidence (Reddit, Substack "how I made $250" posts) suggests the modal/median outcome is far smaller: $0–a few hundred dollars in the first months, with a wide long tail.

### Cited Findings
- **Thomas Frank** (YouTuber, "Ultimate Brain" and "Creator's Companion" Notion templates): cleared **$1,000,508** selling two Notion templates in 2022, with the single product Ultimate Brain accounting for **$760,000** of that; by 2024 his Notion template + education business was generating monthly template revenue averaging **$120,000** plus ~$15,000/month from YouTube AdSense and affiliate deals — but this rode on a pre-existing YouTube channel (his second, template/Notion-focused channel has 280K+ subscribers and 14M+ views). — [Typefully thread](https://typefully.com/TomFrankly/dollar1-million-in-notion-template-sales-kuFT0iD); [easy.tools writeup](https://www.easy.tools/blog/thomas-frank); [Built by Foundry profile](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/thomas-frank-notion-templates-2m-creator-business)
- **Jason Chin ("Easlo")**: publicly stated on X (then-Twitter, Oct 2022) "I earn $20k/mo passive income selling Notion templates. It took me 2 years of hard work and consistency to get there." Multiple secondary write-ups describe cumulative earnings of $500,000+ and ~$200,000/year at points; he started at 18 as a college student in 2020 building templates for organizing college/personal-finance/health, and grew a following alongside the templates. Treat the specific cumulative totals ($500K+) as secondary-source aggregation of his own claims, not independently audited, but the $20K/mo figure is a direct, named, dated self-report. — [X/Twitter post](https://x.com/heyeaslo/status/1578022305946443778); [secondary case study](https://medium.com/@hey.musli/case-study-1-easlo-21-years-old-earned-over-500k-from-selling-notion-templates-bdf5f086ab43); [LinkedIn summary](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/michelle-marcelline_6-figure-income-age-21-no-coding-skill-activity-7117694862107279360-j3Ml)
- **Modest Mitkus / Pathpages (formerly "Notionway")**: founder account (via MailerLite case study and Starter Story interview) reports the business at **$20.8K/month** in revenue, **$300,000+** cumulative over a few years, run essentially solo. Distribution is explicitly credited to email marketing — a weekly newsletter reaching ~100,000 people and social channels totaling ~200,000 followers, plus 120,000+ people who have used his templates. Started in 2021 after leaving a design-agency career. This is a named, first-person account (interview/case-study format), not independently audited, but concrete and consistent across two separate write-ups. — [MailerLite case study](https://www.mailerlite.com/case-studies/notionway); [Starter Story interview](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/pathpages)
- **Modest Mitkus / "$100,000+ Selling Notion Templates" account**: separately describes starting with 0 followers, 0 traffic, $0 and reaching $100,000 in sales — cited as a "no existing audience at launch" case, though the same person subsequently built a large newsletter/social following that now drives the bulk of recurring revenue (see above). — [Goodreads-hosted blog post](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/23677456-100-000-selling-notion-templates)
- **Emily McDermott**, Etsy seller of spreadsheet templates: self-reported **$700 in her first month**, scaling to **$7,000/month within three months** by adding products and bundles; a separate/later self-account claims **$280,000 cumulative** from spreadsheet sales on Etsy. — [Medium first-person post](https://emily-mcdermott.medium.com/how-ive-made-280k-selling-spreadsheets-on-etsy-51b0759a9465)
- **"SimplyOrganizedPro"** (Etsy shop, per secondary aggregator coverage, not a first-person account): started 2022, **37,000+ sales across 24 products**, estimated cumulative revenue around **$160,000** over ~2 years; best-selling product (Monthly Budget Spreadsheet) had 8,600+ sales at $1–$2.50 each — illustrating a high-volume/low-price model. — [Goodreads-hosted summary](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24510163-160k-in-2-years-selling-spreadsheet-templates)
- A separate secondary account describes a spreadsheet-template seller reaching **$40,000/month via Pinterest** as the primary traffic driver (not a named individual with independent verification found). — [Goodreads-hosted summary](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25460887-40-000-per-month-selling-spreadsheet-templates-via-pinterest)
- Small/failure-adjacent, first-person Substack accounts: one creator reports **$250 total** in the first ~2 months after starting in July 2024, with the first two months at **$0 revenue**; another reports **$847 in 30 days** from 3 templates; one Reddit-sourced claim (via secondary aggregation, unverified) of **$3,000 in the first month**. — [themindfulplanner Substack](https://themindfulplanner.substack.com/p/how-i-made-250-selling-notion-templates); [secondary aggregation citing Reddit and the $847/30-day figure](https://kupkaike.com/blog/how-to-sell-notion-templates-gumroad-2026) (kupkaike.com itself could not be directly fetched due to network access restrictions — findings here are from search-result summaries, treat as lower-confidence)
- A solo Gumroad ebook seller's month-by-month income tracker (self-reported on Medium): grew from **$127 in January 2024** to **$2,539 in December 2024** — a relevant proxy for typical Gumroad digital-product trajectories even though the product category (ebooks) differs slightly from templates. — [Medium income breakdown](https://medium.com/write-a-catalyst/my-gumroad-income-breakdown-2-539-in-revenue-heres-what-worked-c0797855df76)
- Platform-level context: Gumroad publicly stated it passed **$1 billion cumulative creator earnings** in 2024, and charges a flat 10% transaction fee; this is platform marketing/PR framing, not evidence about individual template sellers specifically. — [Gumroad X/Twitter post](https://x.com/gumroad/status/1742642509401202936)

### Inferences
- The "named, large, verifiable" cases cluster around people who either had or built substantial owned audiences (YouTube for Thomas Frank, Twitter/X following for Easlo, an email newsletter of ~100K for Pathpages, Pinterest SEO traffic for the spreadsheet sellers). Pure "build a great template with zero distribution and let the platform market discovery sell it" stories with six-figure results are not found in these searches — even the "0 followers, 0 traffic, $100K" framing (Modest Mitkus) is from someone who went on to build a large audience, and the headline figure's time horizon/attribution is unclear (likely benefited from platform-SEO discovery on Gumroad/Notion marketplace plus early-mover timing in ~2021, which is harder to replicate in 2025-2026).
- The gap between "$20K-$120K+/month" headline names and "$250-$3,000 total" Substack/Reddit accounts is enormous, suggesting a power-law distribution typical of creator economy products: a small number of well-marketed creators capture most of the visible revenue, while a much larger number of quieter sellers generate low-to-moderate side income.

### Gaps
- No independently audited (e.g., tax filings, platform-verified payout screenshots checked by a journalist) figures were found for any of these cases; all are self-reported or reported via marketing-adjacent "case study" blogs (MailerLite, Starter Story, Goodreads-hosted author blog posts) that have an interest in showcasing success.
- Could not directly access stealwhatworks.com or kupkaike.com (blocked by network egress proxy), which appeared in search results as sources specifically analyzing "which Notion templates make $10K+/month" — only got secondary summaries via search snippets, which is a real limitation on precision here.

## What's the realistic revenue range and timeline? What differentiates rare big winners from the median seller?

### Takeaway
For someone starting from zero (no audience, first template), a realistic outcome in the first 1-3 months is $0 to a few hundred dollars; reaching a genuinely meaningful side income ($500-$3,000/month) appears to take 3-6+ months of consistent output and active marketing, and is far from guaranteed; $10K+/month is rare and, per available evidence, essentially always accompanied by either a pre-built audience or years of compounding content/SEO/email-list effort.

### Cited Findings
- Search-result synthesis (citing kupkaike.com, not independently verified by direct fetch) states: "most creators who put out their first template, share it once on Reddit or Twitter, and do nothing else will earn somewhere between $0 and $100 in the first month," while "top sellers clear $10,000 a month or more" and "most sellers who stick with it land somewhere between $500 and $3,000," with "most creators still active at day 90 earning $200-$800/month." — [kupkaike.com via search summary](https://kupkaike.com/blog/how-to-sell-notion-templates-gumroad-2026) (indirect; site itself blocked from direct fetch)
- On Etsy specifically, one synthesis states meaningful income ($500+/month) "typically takes 3-6 months and 15-30 listings," with category-level realistic monthly ranges cited as: Budget Planner Printables $300-$2,000; Meal Planning Printables $200-$1,500; Daily Planner Pages $400-$3,000; Weekly Planner Printables $300-$2,500 (source aggregation, not an individually named seller — treat as industry-blog estimate, not hard data). — [Printify/ecommerce blog aggregation via search summary]
- Concrete named/dated example of typical early trajectory: Etsy seller Emily McDermott made **$700 in month 1**, reaching **$7,000/month by month 3** — on the faster end of self-reported outcomes, explicitly attributed to "expanding her product range and offering bundles." — [Medium](https://emily-mcdermott.medium.com/how-ive-made-280k-selling-spreadsheets-on-etsy-51b0759a9465)
- A named Notion-template Substack account: **$0 revenue for the first two months**, ~$250 total by month 3 (July-Sept 2024) — a much slower, more typical-looking trajectory. — [Substack](https://themindfulplanner.substack.com/p/how-i-made-250-selling-notion-templates)
- What differentiates the top tier per multiple secondary sources: consistent, multi-channel promotion (Reddit's r/Notion at 300K+ members, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, and especially owned email lists) sustained over months/years, plus building a catalog/bundle of multiple products rather than relying on one template, rather than one-off posting. Pathpages explicitly credits "email marketing as core" to reaching 5-figure monthly revenue. — [MailerLite case study](https://www.mailerlite.com/case-studies/notionway); search synthesis on differentiators
- Numeric framing for how the $10K/month threshold is reached: at premium pricing, a $79 template needs ~127 monthly purchases to cross $10K/month, a $99 bundle needs ~102 — i.e., $10K/month is attainable with a few hundred transactions/month, but sustaining that volume requires ongoing traffic, not a one-time launch spike. — [search synthesis citing stealwhatworks.com, indirect]

### Inferences
- Revenue realistically follows two very different curves depending on whether the seller commits to ongoing marketing (multi-channel posting, email list building, expanding a product catalog) versus a "make once, list once" approach — the latter plausibly tops out around $0-$500 total based on available first-person accounts.
- Because several of the clearest $10K+/month case studies (Thomas Frank, Easlo, Pathpages) took roughly **1-3 years** of consistent output before reaching that level, near-term (first-year) expectations for a brand-new, audience-less seller should be modest — low hundreds to low thousands of dollars total, not monthly, in year one, unless the seller invests heavily and early in audience-building channels (which is itself a different skill set than template-making).

### Gaps
- No large-sample, platform-level dataset (e.g., Gumroad or Etsy publishing a seller-income distribution/percentile breakdown for the template/digital-download category specifically) was found; all "median/typical" figures come from independent bloggers' estimates or aggregated Reddit impressions, not verifiable platform statistics.

## What skills actually matter (design taste, marketing/distribution, niche selection) versus what doesn't (coding)?

### Takeaway
Coding is essentially irrelevant — Notion template-building, Figma templates, and Etsy printables all require no code, and multiple sources explicitly note templates can be built using only Notion itself (free) or AI tools, with "zero design skills" cited as workable. What matters most, per the available case studies, is sustained marketing/distribution (especially owned channels: email lists, social following, SEO/Pinterest) and niche/product selection (bundling, multiple price points, catalog breadth) — design polish matters for conversion but is consistently described as secondary to distribution.

### Cited Findings
- Multiple how-to sources state templates can be built "without design software, coding skills, or expensive tools" using Notion itself (free) or AI assistance; one Medium post is explicitly titled "How I Used AI to Create Stunning Notion Templates (Without Any Design Skills!)." — [feather.so guide](https://feather.so/blog/how-to-create-a-notion-template); [Medium AI-design post](https://medium.com/@professional.shorya/how-i-used-ai-to-create-stunning-notion-templates-without-any-design-skills-31aecee7c534)
- "Your marketing strategy determines whether you make sales" — search synthesis across multiple guides consistently frames marketing/promotion as the primary sales driver, more than product quality alone, once a baseline of functional design is met.
- Pathpages/Modest Mitkus case explicitly attributes success to email marketing as "core" and a catalog approach (templates at multiple price points/entry points) rather than a single hit product. — [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/case-studies/notionway)
- Thomas Frank's bundle (Ultimate Brain + Creator's Companion) "outsells standalone products at a 3:1 ratio" — evidence that product-line/catalog strategy (a skill closer to product/marketing strategy than design or coding) materially affects revenue. — [easy.tools blog](https://www.easy.tools/blog/thomas-frank)
- One synthesis explicitly frames the key differentiator between low and high earners as marketing effort/consistency (posting across Reddit, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, email) rather than template sophistication. — search synthesis, indirect (kupkaike.com)

### Inferences
- This income stream inverts the typical assumption that design/polish is the bottleneck: the binding constraint is distribution capability (building or accessing an audience, consistent multi-channel promotion, email-list ownership) and business/product strategy (bundling, pricing, catalog breadth), not craft skill. This matches patterns seen in other creator-economy digital products.
- Because coding is not required at all, this reduces one barrier the user asked about, but the researched evidence suggests the *actual* bottleneck (marketing/distribution) may be just as hard to acquire for someone with "no existing audience," arguably harder than learning design.

### Gaps
- No source directly quantifies the relative revenue contribution of "design quality" versus "marketing effort" in a controlled way (e.g., A/B testing on identical templates) — the marketing-primacy conclusion is an inference from pattern-matching across case studies, not a directly cited causal study.

## How saturated is the Notion template market in 2025-2026 — is it still viable for a new entrant?

### Takeaway
Evidence on saturation is explicitly mixed even within search results from 2025-2026-dated sources: some describe the market as saturated, others claim it is "anything but saturated" and still growing with Notion's user base. First-person accounts describe early discouragement (feeling saturated, doubting they could stand out) that some pushed through with persistence and differentiation, while others stayed at very low revenue.

### Cited Findings
- One 2025/2026-dated source states: "The market for Notion-related product sales looks saturated in 2025." — search synthesis, specific source URL not resolved in results
- A contrasting source states: "The market for Notion templates has grown steadily since 2020, and it's still far from saturated" and "As the user base of Notion is growing, the opportunities for Notion template creators are rising as well, and the market is anything but saturated." — search synthesis, specific source URL not resolved in results
- A first-person account (themindfulplanner Substack) describes early hopelessness — "beginning to lose all hope, thinking they'd gone into a business model that was too saturated, and that they'd never stand out" — before eventually reaching modest success (~$250). — [Substack](https://themindfulplanner.substack.com/p/how-i-made-250-selling-notion-templates)
- Synthesis framing: "the Notion template market rewards people who show up consistently and improve over time" — i.e., sources framing saturation as a barrier that consistency/differentiation can overcome, rather than a hard ceiling — but this framing comes predominantly from guide/how-to content that has an incentive to encourage new entrants (some are themselves selling courses or templates about selling templates), which is a conflict of interest worth flagging.

### Inferences
- The "market is saturated" vs. "market is growing" claims are likely both partially true simultaneously: the *general-purpose* productivity/planner template niche (habit trackers, budget planners, daily planners) is crowded with low-differentiation entrants, while narrower or newer niches (specific professional workflows, niche hobbies, non-English languages, HWP/Korean document templates, emerging tool categories) may still have room — but no source directly substantiates this niche-vs-generic distinction with data.
- Given that many "how to sell Notion templates" guides are themselves monetized content (selling courses, affiliate links, or templates about template-selling), the sunny "market isn't saturated" framing should be discounted as having a promotional bias; the more credible middle-ground reading is that broad/generic template categories are increasingly crowded, and new entrants without differentiation or distribution face longer odds than the 2020-2022 cohort (Easlo, Thomas Frank, Mitkus) did.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found (e.g., number of active Notion template listings over time, average listing view-to-sale conversion rate trend, marketplace new-listing growth rate) to actually measure saturation. This is a significant gap — all claims here are qualitative and often come from interested parties (guide sellers).
- No comparable saturation discussion was found specifically for Figma templates, checklist/worksheet packs, or HWP (Korean document format) templates — the search coverage here is heavily Notion- and Etsy-planner-skewed; HWP-specific seller income data was not found at all in this research pass.

## Does an existing audience/following matter as much here as in SaaS case studies?

### Takeaway
Yes — arguably more so. Every large, verifiable, named case in this research (Thomas Frank, Easlo, Modest Mitkus/Pathpages, and the Pinterest-driven spreadsheet sellers) either started with or rapidly built a large owned audience (YouTube subscribers, X/Twitter following, email newsletter, or Pinterest SEO traffic) that became the dominant sales channel, closely paralleling the audience-dependency seen in SaaS "build in public" case studies.

### Cited Findings
- Thomas Frank's template revenue is explicitly tied to a dedicated 280K+ subscriber YouTube channel built around Notion content. — [builtbyfoundry.io](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/thomas-frank-notion-templates-2m-creator-business)
- Pathpages/Modest Mitkus credits a ~100,000-person weekly newsletter and ~200,000 combined social following as core growth drivers, with email marketing described as "core" to reaching 5-figure monthly revenue. — [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/case-studies/notionway)
- Easlo built his X/Twitter following concurrently with his template business over "2 years of hard work and consistency" before reaching $20K/month — audience-building and product-building happened together, not product first. — [X post](https://x.com/heyeaslo/status/1578022305946443778)
- One spreadsheet-template seller's reported path to $40,000/month is explicitly "via Pinterest" — i.e., an owned/organic traffic channel functioning as the audience substitute. — [Goodreads-hosted summary](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25460887-40-000-per-month-selling-spreadsheet-templates-via-pinterest)
- Counter-evidence/nuance: the "$100,000 selling Notion templates, 0 followers, 0 traffic, $0" framing (same Modest Mitkus) suggests it's possible to start a template business without a pre-existing audience — but this is the same individual who is also the clearest example of an audience-driven business, so the "0 followers" framing likely describes only the very initial launch moment, not the absence of audience-building throughout the described period. — [Goodreads-hosted post](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/23677456-100-000-selling-notion-templates)

### Inferences
- The pattern strongly resembles SaaS "indie hacker" case studies: platform/marketplace discovery (Gumroad search, Notion marketplace, Etsy search) alone is described in guides as capable of producing some organic sales, but every documented large-revenue case layers substantial owned-audience or SEO/paid-traffic-equivalent (Pinterest) distribution on top. A new entrant with literally no audience and no plan to build one should expect outcomes closer to the $0-few-hundred-dollar Substack accounts than to the six-figure named cases.
- Unlike SaaS (where audience can sometimes be substituted by strong SEO/content marketing over a longer runway, or by paid acquisition against a high-LTV product), templates are low-price, largely one-time-purchase items, making sustained top-of-funnel traffic (audience or SEO) even more structurally necessary to reach meaningful recurring revenue, since there's no recurring billing to compound from a smaller customer base.

### Gaps
- No case study was found of someone reaching $5K+/month in templates purely through marketplace/platform algorithmic discovery (Etsy search, Notion marketplace browse, Gumroad discover) with zero external audience-building or SEO content investment — this would be the cleanest test of "does audience matter," and its absence from search results is itself suggestive (though not proof) that this path is rare or unreported.
