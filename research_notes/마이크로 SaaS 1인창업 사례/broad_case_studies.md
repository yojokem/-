# Broad Verified Case Studies: 1–3 Person Micro-SaaS/Small Software Products (2023–2026)

## What concrete, named products/founders have publicly documented their revenue journey?

### Takeaway
At least nine distinct, well-documented, named founder/product cases (2023–2026, with one legacy case — Zigpoll — founded 2019 but still actively reported on in 2023–2025) have public, checkable revenue trajectories via Indie Hackers, Starter Story, founder blogs/X threads, and aggregator teardown sites. Technical backgrounds range from career software engineers (Marc Lou, Tony Dinh, Jason Zigelbaum, Damon Chen, Pieter Levels) to a 25-year veteran ex-startup CTO (Gil Hildebrand) to genuinely non-technical no-code builders (David Bressler, Dan & Sveta Kulkov) — so "you must already be a strong engineer" is not a hard requirement, but the fastest/largest outcomes cluster among people with prior coding or prior startup-operating experience.

### Cited Findings — Case 1: Marc Lou — ShipFast / CodeFast (solo, France → Bali)
- Background: Computer science / technology graduate (Université de Technologie de Troyes), full-stack developer before going independent — [YesPress](https://yespress.io/marc-lou); [FoundersHut](https://www.foundershut.com/explore/marc-lou-shipfast-success)
- Product: ShipFast, a Next.js/boilerplate code-starter kit for developers launching SaaS products faster (dev-tools/boilerplate category)
- Revenue: ShipFast earned $528K in its first 4 months and has crossed $1.23M in all-time Stripe-verified revenue; reported elsewhere as $20K MRR / $100K ARR within 5 months — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/marc-lou-shipfast); [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/marc-lou-shipfast-250k-5-months)
- Scale (2025): $1,032,000 earned in 2025 solo, from a portfolio of 13+ products generating roughly $95K/month combined; zero employees, 91% profit margin — [High Signal](https://www.highsignal.io/how-marc-lou-makes-millions-from-great-marketing/); [Nomadic Blueprint](https://nomadicblueprint.com/case-studies/marc-louvion)
- Hours: Self-reported ~6 hours/day building ("surfing at 7am, shipping by noon, done by 6pm") — [Nomadic Blueprint](https://nomadicblueprint.com/case-studies/marc-louvion)
- Capital: No VC/outside funding mentioned in any source; self-funded from own savings/time only (implicit, not explicitly quantified in sources found)

### Cited Findings — Case 2: Tony Dinh — TypingMind / DevUtils / Xnapper (solo, Vietnam)
- Background: Vietnamese software engineer; quit a full-time software engineering job (reportedly a ~$105K salary role) on Sept 20, 2021 to go independent — [Supabird](https://supabird.io/articles/tony-dinh-from-a-105k-developer-to-a-1-million-indie-hacking-marvel); [OneManDB](https://www.onemandb.com/founder/tony-dinh)
- Product: TypingMind — an alternative chat UI for ChatGPT/LLM APIs (AI productivity tool)
- Time to revenue: Idea conceived March 1, 2023, domain registered next day; hit $10K in cumulative revenue by March 10, 2023 — roughly 9 days from idea to $10K — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/he-made-45k-month-with-chatgpt-tony-dinh-s-story-c40283745c); a later product reportedly hit $22K in 11 days — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tony-dinh-hit-22k-in-11-days-by-decoupling-input-from-output-BX5ZtCp7Z4krbLue6qtJ)
- Scale: TypingMind alone ~$130K–$160K/month by October 2025 (>$1M annualized); combined portfolio (TypingMind + DevUtils + Xnapper) averaging $45K/month — [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/tony-dinh-typingmind-22k-7-days); [Supabird](https://supabird.io/articles/tony-dinh-from-a-105k-developer-to-a-1-million-indie-hacking-marvel)
- Other exits in portfolio: "Black Magic" product reached $14K MRR then sold for $128,000; Xnapper (screenshot tool) sold for $150,000 — [Supabird](https://supabird.io/articles/tony-dinh-from-a-105k-developer-to-a-1-million-indie-hacking-marvel)
- Gap: Specific weekly-hours figure and initial capital not found in sources reviewed.

### Cited Findings — Case 3: Gil Hildebrand — Subscribr (solo → small team, YouTube-script AI tool)
- Background: 25 years of coding experience; previously 9 years as CTO of Seth Godin's Squidoo, then founder of a venture-backed crypto SaaS that reached >$1M ARR / 20+ employees but never turned a profit (prior startup failure/lesson) — [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/gil-hildebrand-subscribr-10k-mrr)
- Product: Subscribr — AI tool to plan, brainstorm, and script YouTube videos (creator-economy SaaS)
- Time to revenue: Presold 50 lifetime deals to a pre-existing 1,000-person email list before/at launch (April 2024 public launch); reached $10K MRR within 100 days of launch — [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/gil-hildebrand-subscribr-10k-mrr)
- Initial capital: Not a cash outlay — used a presale/lifetime-deal strategy against an existing audience as "non-dilutive seed funding," explicitly avoiding VC — [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/gil-hildebrand-subscribr-10k-mrr)
- Scale: ~$62K/month reported at one point; ~1.5 years post-launch had ~4,000 customers and had generated over $500K in 2025 alone — [note.com interview](https://note.com/genai_kuro_biz/n/n3f73a249ea5f?hl=en); [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/gil-hildebrand-subscribr-10k-mrr)
- Notable detail: Now has an AI coding assistant write ~90% of the code (illustrates shrinking technical-skill barrier by 2025) — [note.com](https://note.com/genai_kuro_biz/n/n3f73a249ea5f?hl=en)

### Cited Findings — Case 4: Gaurav & Joe — Fastlane (2-person team, AI short-form marketing tool)
- Background: Gaurav described as a "solo builder turned co-founder"; inspired to start building apps after watching a Starter Story video in mid-2025 — implies a relatively new/less-veteran technical path, though exact prior credentials not detailed in sources — [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/the-1myear-saas-secret-isnt-ai)
- Product: Fastlane — AI short-form video/marketing content tool for indie developers and solopreneurs (marketing-tech category), with a "Tinder-style" swipe UI
- Timeline: Development began October 2025; beta shipped to a 2,000-person waitlist by December 2025; public launch March 23, 2026 — [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/this-tinder-ui-makes-70kmonth)
- Time to revenue: Reached $69,000 MRR barely two months after public launch, with 1,000+ paid users and >$1M annualized revenue — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/the-insanely-obvious-secret-behind-this-69k-month-saas)
- Method: ~2,000 customer discovery calls conducted since August 2025 (about 20/week), which drove a pivot from a broad horizontal tool to the vertical short-form product and the UI overhaul — [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/422a5962ca9b9dcb)
- Gap: Initial capital and weekly building hours not disclosed in sources found.

### Cited Findings — Case 5: Dan & Sveta Kulkov — FounderPal (2-person/couple team, no-code)
- Background: Dan Kulkov previously worked as a COO in EdTech/HR startups — an operations/business background, not a traditional engineering one; built with his wife Sveta after both quit corporate jobs — [nocodeexits.substack.com](https://nocodeexits.substack.com/p/how-dan-kulkov-grew-founderpal-to)
- Product: FounderPal — AI marketing-strategy generator for solopreneurs (built via pivots from a tweet-idea generator, then an AI marketing-coaching product)
- Tools: Built with Bubble (no-code) for front end and back end, later added Xano as backend — explicitly a no-code build, no traditional programming — [nocodeexits.substack.com](https://nocodeexits.substack.com/p/how-dan-kulkov-grew-founderpal-to)
- Revenue path: First pivot ("tweet ideas generator") made only ~$1,000 before being abandoned; after 2 pivots, the AI Marketing Strategy Generator made $100,000+ in its first year; over 19 months, total revenue reached $126K — [X/Dan Kulkov](https://x.com/DanKulkov/status/1886323998805700912); [thesuccessfulprojects.com](https://www.thesuccessfulprojects.com/case-study-9-how-this-couple-made-60k-helping-solopreneurs-in-marketing-with-an-ai-wrapper-founderpal-ai-10k-mrr/)
- Current scale: More recent figures show FounderPal at roughly $1.8K/month — i.e., revenue declined significantly from its 2024 peak, a useful contrast case showing micro-SaaS revenue is not always monotonically increasing — [Indie Hackers product page](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/founderpal/revenue)
- Gap: Weekly hours and specific up-front capital not disclosed (no-code tooling costs likely modest, i.e. Bubble/Xano subscription tier, but no dollar figure found).

### Cited Findings — Case 6: Jason Zigelbaum — Zigpoll (solo, Shopify-feedback/survey tool)
- Background: ~10 years as a front-end developer at an e-commerce agency and Head of Front End Web Development at BORN Group; M.S. in Computer Science, Washington University in St. Louis (2007–2012) — fully technical background — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/jason-zigelbaum-90ad)
- Prior track record: Built and exited earlier Shopify-adjacent apps (Metafields Manager, absorbed by Shopify; another app sold); ran a COVID-era virtual concert platform that generated ~$400K in revenue — [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/jason-zigelbaum-zigpoll-125k-mrr-shopify-feedback-surveys)
- Time to revenue: Zigpoll acquired its first paying customer in 7 days — [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/jason-zigelbaum-zigpoll-125k-mrr-shopify-feedback-surveys)
- Time to meaningful traction: Took about 2 years to get real traction, but then doubled revenue every year since — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/hitting-125k-mrr-as-a-solo-founder-by-doubling-down-on-the-right-segment-c4o2Tfs6mjdpip5yZhaO)
- Scale: $125K MRR as a fully solo operator — no cofounder, no funding, no sales team; built the first version himself with a code editor — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/hitting-125k-mrr-as-a-solo-founder-by-doubling-down-on-the-right-segment-c4o2Tfs6mjdpip5yZhaO)
- Note: Zigpoll was founded in 2019 (pre-2023), but its reported $125K MRR milestone and podcast coverage are from 2023–2025, so it's included as a still-relevant, heavily-documented long-tail-success story, flagged as an older founding date per the research constraints.

### Cited Findings — Case 7: David Bressler — Formula Bot / Excelformulabot (solo, non-technical/no-code)
- Background: Digital marketing graduate (not a software engineer); built the product on paternity leave in 2023 with six weeks off and a newborn — explicitly a non-technical founder — [hustlers.beehiiv.com](https://hustlers.beehiiv.com/p/david-ai-excel-formula-bot); [nocode.mba](https://www.nocode.mba/articles/nocode-case-study-formulabot)
- Tools: Built entirely on Bubble (no-code) integrating the OpenAI API, Stripe for payments, SendinBlue for email — [Bubble.io blog](https://bubble.io/blog/formula-bot/)
- Time to revenue: Reached $3K MRR within 2 months of launch via no-code + influencer marketing — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/leveraging-no-code-and-influencers-to-hit-3k-mrr-in-2-months-david-bressler-of-excelformulabot-9ffd4bc22b)
- Early scale: Within 3 months of launch, reached $30K in cumulative revenue, $4.0K MRR, and 1.1M site visitors — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-to-build-a-1m-product-with-no-coding-david-bresslers-story-172bda76ab)
- Later scale: Under 2 years, reached 1 million users; more recent reporting cites ~$2.8M/year revenue (some sources cite peak ~$226K/month) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-to-build-a-1m-product-with-no-coding-david-bresslers-story-172bda76ab); [Willy Shinn](https://www.willyshinn.com/p/the-excel-whisperer-how-david-bressler)
- Capital: Fully bootstrapped, no outside investment stated, though this case is a strong outlier/best-case within no-code stories — most no-code micro-SaaS do not reach this scale.

### Cited Findings — Case 8: Pieter Levels — Photo AI (solo, self-taught, longtime "indie hacker" figure)
- Background: Self-taught programmer (no formal CS credential referenced); builds everything solo in plain PHP + SQLite on a single Hetzner server — deliberately low-tech/low-cost stack; codebase reported as a single ~40,870-line index.php file — [aituts.com](https://aituts.com/case-study/photo-ai-pieter-levels/); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579)
- Product: Photo AI — AI-generated photo/headshot product (consumer AI image-gen SaaS), launched Feb 10, 2023
- Time to revenue: ~$5.4K MRR in the first week of launch — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579)
- Growth curve (documented): Month 2 ≈ $28.7K MRR; Month 6 ≈ $61.8K MRR; Month 12 ≈ $77K MRR; Month 18 > $100K–$132K MRR — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579); [andrew.ooo](https://andrew.ooo/posts/pieter-levels-photo-ai-solo-founder/)
- More recent figure: ~$105K–$150K/month revenue reported in 2026 reporting, with 87% profit margin and ~2,573 active subscribers at one snapshot — [andrew.ooo](https://andrew.ooo/posts/pieter-levels-photo-ai-solo-founder/)
- Capital: 100% bootstrapped, $0 external funding; his key asymmetric advantage was a pre-built 600K-follower Twitter/X audience cultivated over ~10 years of building 40+ products, which materially lowers customer-acquisition cost and is not available to a brand-new founder — [Tycoon.us](https://tycoon.us/case-studies/pieter-levels)
- Distribution caveat: This case should be read with the caveat that distribution advantage (a decade-old large audience) is doing significant work alongside the technical build — generalizing the "time to $100K MRR" timeline to a founder without an existing audience is likely overly optimistic.

### Cited Findings — Case 9: Damon Chen — PDF.ai (and separately, Testimonial.to) — solo, engineer-turned-founder via acquisition
- Background: Software engineer, previously worked at Cisco — [LinkedIn via search summary](https://www.linkedin.com/in/damengchen/)
- Notable path: Rather than building from scratch, Damon acquired an existing pre-revenue weekend project (then called looseleaf.ai, $0 revenue) for $20,000 in May 2023 and rebuilt/relaunched it as PDF.ai — an "acqui-build" rather than a from-scratch launch — [SaaS Starter Stack interview](https://saasstarterstack.com/interviews/pdfai)
- Growth: By September 2023 (a few months post-acquisition), PDF.ai reached $25,070 MRR with $17,600 profit and 1,047,000 monthly site visitors — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/pdf-ai-tool-made-25k-in-mrr-last-month-07148fa17d)
- Scale: Grew beyond $50K MRR; 2024 estimated revenue ~$591.7K/year (bootstrapped, no VC) per third-party revenue-tracking site — [Getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/pdf.ai)
- Separately, Damon Chen is also publicly associated with Testimonial.to, which reached $100K ARR in 9 months as a solo project and grew to an estimated $2.4M ARR in 2024 (up from $420K in 2023), fully bootstrapped — [growthpartners.online](https://growthpartners.online/stories/how-testimonial-to-hit-100k-arr); [Getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/testimonial-io)
- Note: Sources conflict somewhat on exactly how Damon Chen's involvement in Testimonial.to vs. PDF.ai is structured (founder vs. later operator); the $20K acquisition price and the Sept-2023 $25K MRR figure for PDF.ai are the most concretely sourced data points and are flagged as most reliable.

### Inferences
- Time-to-first-revenue in these documented cases ranges from "days" (Tony Dinh: ~9 days to $10K cumulative; Jason Zigelbaum: 7 days to first customer) to "a couple of months" (Subscribr: 100 days to $10K MRR; Fastlane: ~2 months to $69K MRR; Formula Bot: 2 months to $3K MRR) — with Zigpoll as an outlier requiring ~2 years to reach real traction despite an experienced technical founder, showing high variance even among skilled operators.
- Nearly every fast/large outcome in this set had either (a) a strong pre-existing technical background enabling rapid, low-cost solo building (Levels, Dinh, Zigelbaum, Chen), (b) a pre-existing audience/email list used for a presale that functioned as de facto non-dilutive capital (Hildebrand's 1,000-person list → 50 lifetime deals), or (c) heavy use of no-code tooling (Bubble + Xano) by a founder with zero coding background (Bressler, the Kulkovs) — suggesting multiple distinct viable paths rather than one dominant formula.
- No-code (primarily Bubble, sometimes paired with Xano) is the concrete, named platform repeatedly cited as letting non-technical/self-taught founders ship a real, revenue-generating product without hiring a developer; AI coding assistants (mentioned explicitly by Hildebrand, who has AI write ~90% of Subscribr's code by 2025) appear to be displacing no-code platforms as the low-skill-founder enabler in the most recent (2025–2026) cases.
- Revenue is not always monotonic: FounderPal's decline from a ~$100K first year to ~$1.8K/month in more recent reporting is a documented contrary data point that tempers "is once = stays" survivorship narratives.

### Gaps
- Weekly-hours figures during the early building phase were explicitly documented for only one case in detail (Marc Lou, ~6 hrs/day self-reported); other founders (Dinh, Hildebrand, Zigelbaum, Gaurav/Joe, the Kulkovs, Bressler, Chen) did not have a specific early-phase weekly-hour commitment found in the sources reviewed — flagged as a gap across the set.
- Initial capital/up-front cash outlay is explicit for only two cases: PDF.ai ($20,000 acquisition price) and Subscribr (no cash outlay — used a presale/lifetime-deal strategy instead of capital). For the rest (Lou, Dinh, Zigelbaum, Gaurav/Joe, Kulkovs, Bressler, Levels) no specific dollar figure for starting capital was found; most sources imply "bootstrapped from personal savings/side income" without quantifying it.
- The earlier search-tool summary surfaced an unverifiable claim about a "PDFPro" ($28K MRR in 11 months, two founders) that could not be traced to a primary source on a second, more targeted search — this claim is explicitly NOT included above as a case study because it could not be verified, and is noted here only as a caution against trusting first-pass aggregator summaries without confirming the primary source.
- Could not verify a clean median/typical "time to first dollar" figure directly from IndieHackers' own published aggregate data (only third-party summarizations citing "3-4 months" for a "median bootstrapped micro SaaS" were found — see the broader IndieHackers/SaaS-market statistics topic, which is likely covered by a separate parallel research stream on aggregate statistics rather than named case studies).

## How much variance is there in "time to meaningful revenue" across these cases?

### Takeaway
Variance is extremely high — from under 10 days (Tony Dinh, Jason Zigelbaum's first customer) to roughly 2 years for genuine traction (Zigelbaum's case again, illustrating that "first dollar" and "meaningful/durable revenue" are very different milestones) — and the fastest cases are disproportionately driven by founders who already had a technical skill set, a pre-built audience, or both, rather than by the product idea alone.

### Cited Findings
- Tony Dinh: idea to domain registration in 1 day; domain to $10K cumulative revenue in ~9 days — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/he-made-45k-month-with-chatgpt-tony-dinh-s-story-c40283745c)
- Jason Zigelbaum: first paying customer within 7 days of building Zigpoll, but "about two years to get traction" for durable/meaningful revenue — [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/jason-zigelbaum-zigpoll-125k-mrr-shopify-feedback-surveys); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/hitting-125k-mrr-as-a-solo-founder-by-doubling-down-on-the-right-segment-c4o2Tfs6mjdpip5yZhaO)
- Gil Hildebrand / Subscribr: $10K MRR within 100 days of public launch (helped substantially by a presale to an existing list before that clock even started) — [startupfounderstories.com](https://startupfornderstories.com/stories/gil-hildebrand-subscribr-10k-mrr)
- Gaurav & Joe / Fastlane: ~2 months from public launch to $69K MRR, but preceded by ~5 months of development plus ~2,000 customer-discovery calls before that launch — [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/the-1myear-saas-secret-isnt-ai); [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/the-insanely-obvious-secret-behind-this-69k-month-saas)
- David Bressler / Formula Bot: $3K MRR within 2 months of launch — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/leveraging-no-code-and-influencers-to-hit-3k-mrr-in-2-months-david-bressler-of-excelformulabot-9ffd4bc22b)
- Pieter Levels / Photo AI: $5.4K MRR in the first week — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579)
- Third-party aggregate claim (not a named case, use with caution): "the median bootstrapped micro SaaS earns its first dollar within 3–4 months of starting development" — [betterlaunch.co](https://www.betterlaunch.co/blog/indie-hacker) (this is a secondary/aggregator claim, not independently verified against primary data, and is a different metric — "median across all attempts" — than the named success-case timelines above, which are survivorship-biased by definition)

### Inferences
- "First dollar" and "meaningful/sustainable revenue" should be tracked as two separate milestones — several of these founders hit a first sale or a short-term revenue spike within days to weeks, but converting that into durable $10K+ MRR took anywhere from ~1 month (Levels, arguably) to ~2 years (Zigelbaum).
- The single largest external variable correlating with speed was pre-existing distribution (an audience, an email list, or a fast organic/viral moment) rather than the product-build time itself, which in nearly every case was measured in weeks, not months.

### Gaps
- No case in this set gives a precise day-by-day breakdown distinguishing "time spent coding the MVP" from "time spent on marketing/customer discovery before launch," which limits precise apples-to-apples comparison of build time alone.

## What tools/no-code platforms (if any) let low-skill founders ship something real?

### Takeaway
Bubble (paired with Xano for backend/API needs) is the concretely named, repeatedly-verified no-code platform behind two of this set's best-documented non-technical success stories (Formula Bot, FounderPal); by 2025–2026, AI coding assistants are additionally cited as letting even a technically able founder (Hildebrand) multiply output by having AI write ~90% of the code, which functionally lowers the skill floor further even for people who could code manually.

### Cited Findings
- David Bressler (Formula Bot) — no formal coding background, built entirely in Bubble (front end + back end), with Stripe for payments and SendinBlue for email automation — [Bubble.io official blog](https://bubble.io/blog/formula-bot/); [nocode.mba](https://www.nocode.mba/articles/nocode-case-study-formulabot)
- Dan & Sveta Kulkov (FounderPal) — Dan's background is COO/operations, not engineering; built the first version in Bubble, later added Xano as a backend layer — [nocodeexits.substack.com](https://nocodeexits.substack.com/p/how-dan-kulkov-grew-founderpal-to)
- Gil Hildebrand (Subscribr) — technically skilled (25 years coding) but by 2025 has an AI coding assistant write ~90% of the codebase, illustrating the AI-assisted-coding path as a parallel/successor trend to no-code platforms for lowering the effective skill barrier — [note.com interview](https://note.com/genai_kuro_biz/n/n3f73a249ea5f?hl=en)
- General market-level claim (secondary, not tied to a specific named case above): among recent product launches, the split between traditional coding and no-code/AI-assisted tools is estimated at roughly 50/50 for 2024–2025 launches — [WebSearch summary citing unnamed secondary source, exact origin unclear] — flagged as unverified/aggregator-level claim, included only as directional context, not as a citable hard statistic.

### Inferences
- Bubble + Xano is the concrete, reproducible no-code stack among the cases here that low-skill founders used to ship an actually-revenue-generating product (not just a prototype) — both named cases using it reached real money ($3K–$4K MRR within 2–3 months, eventually $100K+ cumulative revenue for Formula Bot).
- The frontier is shifting: rather than "no-code platform vs. hand-coding" being the main binary, the more recent 2025–2026 cases (Hildebrand) suggest "AI-assisted coding" is becoming the dominant low-skill-founder enabler, potentially superseding pure no-code tools like Bubble because it produces a real, ownable, non-platform-locked codebase.

### Gaps
- Could not find a named 2023–2026 case using other prominent no-code platforms (e.g., Adalo, Glide, Softr, FlutterFlow) that reached comparable, well-documented revenue scale in the sources reviewed within the tool-call budget for this research pass — this may be worth a targeted follow-up search by the report writer or a parallel researcher if platform diversity beyond Bubble is needed.
- Could not verify the specific monthly cost (capital outlay) of running Bubble/Xano at the scale these two products reached, which would help quantify "initial capital required" for the no-code path specifically.
