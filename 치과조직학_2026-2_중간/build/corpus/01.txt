                            Epithelium
• two forms:
   – as sheets of contiguous cells (epithelia)
       • cover the body on its external surface
       • line the body on its internal surface
   – as glands
       • originate from invaginated epithelial cells.
• Origin three embryonic germ layers
   – Ectoderm
       • oral and nasal mucosae, cornea, epidermis of the skin, and glands of
         the skin and the mammary glands.
   – Endoderm:
       • liver, gall bladder, the pancreas, and the lining of the respiratory
         and gastrointestinal tract
   – Mesoderm:
       • uriniferous tubules of the kidney,
       • lining of the male and female reproductive systems,
       • endothelial lining of the circulatory system,
       • mesothelium of the body cavities
Morphology of epithelium

• tightly bound together by
  junctional complexes.
• little intercellular space
• little extracellular matrix.
• basal lamina
   – an extracellular matrix
   – separates from the underlying
     connective tissue
   – synthesized by the epithelial cells.
   – through the basal lamina
       • capillary beds in the adjacent
         supporting connective tissue supplies
         nourishment and oxygen via diffusion.

              Polarity of epithelial cell
• Apical domain : the region of the epithelial cell facing the lumen
    – rich in ion channels, carrier proteins, H+-ATPase, glycoproteins, and
      hydrolytic enzymes, as well as aquaporins
    – the site where regulated secretory products are delivered for release.
    – Microvilli, stereocilia, cilia, and flagella(of spermatozoa).
• Basolateral domain in contact with the basal lamina.
    – junctional specializations
    – intercellular interdigitations.
    – subdivided into two regions:
        • lateral plasma membrane
        • basal plasma membrane.
    – junctional specializations and receptors for hormones and
      neurotransmitters.
    – rich in Na+/K+-ATPase and ion channels
    – sites for constitutive secretion.
• apical and basolateral domains are separated from each other by
  tight junctions

                                Cilia
  • Movement of the cilia
     – dynein arms slide toward the tip of the cilium.
     – nexin restrains this action
     – translating the sliding movement into a bending motion.
     – the elastic protein complex is stretched.
     – dynein arms release their hold on the B subunit,
     – the elastic protein complex returns to its original length,
     – snapping the cilium back to its straight position (which does not
       require energy),
     – move material at the tip of the cilium.

    dynein
     – susceptible to lung infections
     – males with this syndrome are sterile since their sperm are
       immotile.
